Home Affairs One

Build-cost audit

What it costs to build an app like this.

Three price points, from a clickable concept to a service for the whole country. Ranges are in rand and exclude VAT.

Prepared 22 November 2026 · Concept prototype

The short answer

Each step costs ten to fifteen times the one before.

The step is not more screens. It is connecting to real systems, proving the service is secure, and running it for millions of people.

A lighter option sits below Tier 1: a pitch prototype of about 20 key screens in a design file, with no code, at R250k – R700k over 3–5 weeks.

Salary surveys for 2026 put senior developers in South Africa at roughly R50,000 to R100,000 a month. Agencies bill at about twice the cost of employment, and a team blends senior, mid-level and junior people. Directory listings for South African agencies mostly show US$25–$49 an hour. Contingency is 15% for the pilot and 20% for the national service.

Replace these rates with real supplier rates before using any figure in a proposal. Every total below scales with them.

Sensitivity

What moves the number most.

  1. Connecting to existing systems

    The largest unknown. Each system needs an owner, a tested interface and a fallback for when it is down.

  2. How strongly identity must be proven

    A verified account costs far more than a basic one. Biometric checks cost more again.

  3. Native apps or web only

    Two app stores add build, testing and release work to every change.

  4. Languages

    Eleven official languages multiply content, testing and support.

  5. Low-data and offline use

    Worth it for reach. It adds engineering and testing.

  6. Security and assurance

    Independent testing, monitoring and audit recur every year.

  7. Pace

    Running teams in parallel shortens the calendar and raises the cost.

  8. Procurement route

    One prime contractor, several specialists, or an in-house team each price differently.

Scope

What these figures leave out.

  • Modernising the back-office systems the service would connect to
  • Hardware, connectivity and kiosks at service points
  • Staff training and change management
  • Legal drafting and any regulatory change
  • Contact-centre staff
  • Public awareness and marketing
  • VAT

A note on method

What AI-assisted delivery changes.

This prototype was built with AI assistance in days, not the 10–14 weeks estimated above for a conventional team. That saving is real at Tier 1, where the work is design exploration, front-end code, tests and documents.

It does not carry through in proportion. In Tiers 2 and 3 most of the cost is integration, security assurance, research with real people, governance and operations. A cautious planning assumption is 10–25% off the engineering effort, not off the total.

Next step

From estimate to quote.

A 6–8 week discovery, at about R1.2m – R2.5m, would replace these ranges with a priced proposal. It would confirm the scope of the first release, test each integration with its system owner, settle the security architecture and procurement route, and produce a delivery plan.

Read this before quoting a number

Limits of this audit.

  • The effort figures are judgement, built from the size of this prototype and the brief's scope. They have not been checked against supplier quotes.
  • The rates rest on public salary surveys and directory listings, which are self-reported and vary in method.
  • No exchange rate is applied. Directory rates are in US dollars as found.
  • The national tier is the least certain. Treat it as an order of magnitude.
  • Nothing here describes what any real Home Affairs system costs.
