The opportunity

Why one front door, and why it is worth more than an app.

A service finder, an application desk, a status board, a secure inbox and a help line in one. Every journey that moves into it is one fewer errand done twice.

01

One place to start

People look for what they need to do, such as replacing an ID or registering a birth, not for a form. One catalogue says what happens at each step.

02

Status people can trust

One timeline for each application, driven by what actually happened. "Received" never implies "approved".

03

Fewer wasted trips

Before leaving home, a person knows what to bring, where to go and when. The in-person step is booked, not queued for.

04

One conversation, every channel

The secure inbox holds the detail. SMS, email and WhatsApp only point back to it, with a masked reference.

05

A person, always

Digital is a choice, not a condition. Every journey keeps an in-person route, and a call-back carries the context with it.

06

A channel the department owns

Official notices come from one verified place, so people can tell a real message from a fake one.

Architecture on a page

Screens never touch a system directly. One gateway does.

Four layers, each with one job. A fault in one system shows a clear message and a way forward, not a broken screen.

The apps

Web, iPhone and Android for the public. A separate console for staff. A sandbox for partners.

↓
One gateway

Time limits, retries, protection against double submission, a reference for every request, safe error messages and an audit record.

↓
Nine services, nine contracts

Identity, catalogue, workflow, appointments, notifications, payments, WhatsApp, audit and support.

↓
Department systems

Existing systems and approved providers, each reached through one adapter. Up to six connections in this price.

Swap, do not rewrite

All nine contracts are written and tested today against stand-ins. Connecting a real system changes one adapter. The screens do not change.

  • Work continues while a system owner prepares access
  • Each connection is tested on its own, then switched on
  • A system that is down degrades one feature, not the app

Guardrails, built in

Nine rules the build cannot break.

They come from the brief and bind every change. Several are already held by automated tests, not by convention.

01

No invented facts

Fees, requirements and timeframes come only from content its owner has approved.

02

Synthetic data until go-live

No real records in design, testing or demonstrations.

03

No imitation of official marks

No seals, certificates, cards or codes are drawn or generated.

04

AI assists, never decides

Guidance is quoted with its source. Every decision is recorded by an official.

05

Outside channels carry no detail

A masked reference and a secure link only. Never documents or PINs.

06

Nothing is attested for the person

Declarations need the person's own tick. Nothing submits itself.

07

New capabilities stay switched off

Wallet and biometric features sit behind flags until they are approved.

08

No personal information in logs

Audit records hold opaque references, never names or numbers.

09

Accessibility is a release condition

WCAG 2.2 AA is the target, tested with disabled people before each wave.

The other side of the counter

People decide. The system records.

Officials get their own console, with its own sign-in. It shows the minimum each task needs and records every action.

What it covers

What R41 million covers, and what it does not.

A fixed price is only honest with its edges drawn. These are the edges, and what running the service costs each year after handover.

In the price

  • Discovery, service design and content for thirteen services
  • Web, iPhone and Android apps, and the staff console
  • Sign-in through the department's approved identity provider
  • One integration layer with up to six system connections
  • Eleven official languages
  • Three security test rounds and an accessibility audit
  • Pilot, national launch and three months of close support
  • Source code, documents and runbooks

Not in the price

  • Modernising or replacing back-office systems
  • Hosting, messaging fees and software licences
  • Hardware and connectivity at service points
  • Counter and contact-centre staff
  • Paid media
  • Switching on capabilities that need new legal approval, such as a wallet or biometric checks
  • More than six system connections or thirteen services
  • Running the service after month 18

Protection

How the investment is protected.

Risk How we manage it
Systems differ from our assumption Week-8 gate: owners, interfaces and test access are confirmed before build money is spent, and the scope is reset with you if needed.
A connection is late The app runs against its stand-in until the real system is ready. Other work does not wait.
Security findings Three independent test rounds. Findings are closed before each wave opens, from the risk reserve.
Content is not approved in time Only approved services go live. The catalogue is versioned, so a service is added without a release.
People are left behind Every journey keeps an in-person route, and assisted completion is measured from the pilot.
Scope grows A fixed day rate of R5 520 and written change control. Nothing is added without a price.
We become unavailable Source code, documents and runbooks are handed over at every stage, not only at the end.
