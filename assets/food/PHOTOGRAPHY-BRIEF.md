# Food photography brief — the eight most recent items

The catalogue has 24 products and 24 masters, one each. Sixteen were supplied at
the outset. The eight below were added later, borrowed an image for a while, and
now carry a **generated comp** — good enough to lay out a page and settle a
crop, and not a photograph of product.

This brief holds the prompt each comp was written from, and doubles as the shot
list for replacing them. It is written to *Brand Identity Guidelines v1.0*
§05.2 (Food Photography Standards) and §17 (Food Photography).

**Status: all eight comped, none shot.** Gate 11.1 is not cleared by them.

---

## Two things to check before these go further

**The logos are generated, not the licensed lockup.** Three of the comps carry
bb.q packaging in frame — the burger's fries cup, the sweet potato fries carton,
the ddeok-bokki bowl. The supplied masters carry branding too, so they sit
consistently, but a rendered wordmark is an approximation of the mark and not
the mark. Check each against the guidelines' lockup rules before any of this is
used outside review. This is exactly why the prompts below say *no text, no
logos, no watermarks*.

**Everything else below still applies.**

## Before anything is used

The guidelines say, in §17.2 and again in the closing note: **"Use real food.
Capture the natural texture and appearance"** and *"We photograph food the way
we make it — real, delicious and made with care."*

A generated image is not that. These prompts produce **comps** — placeholders
good enough to lay out a page, size a crop and settle a composition with the
franchisor before a photographer is booked. They must not be presented as
photographs of bb.q Chicken product, and they should be replaced by a real
shoot before launch. §11.1 (UX approval against brand guidelines) is not
cleared by them.

Treat this brief as the shot list for that shoot as much as a prompt file: the
angle, styling and lighting notes are the same either way.

---

## The house style, applied to every prompt

Drawn from §05.2 Style Guidelines and §17.5 Do's:

| Guideline | How it appears in the prompts |
|---|---|
| Natural & warm lighting | "soft warm directional window light from the side", never flash |
| Sharp focus | "razor sharp focus on the food, shallow natural depth of field" |
| Warm colours | deep browns, amber, golden yellows, cream; bb.q Red as the sauce, not a filter |
| Minimal props | one dipping bowl, chopsticks, a linen napkin — nothing more |
| Clean background | "clean uncluttered deep charcoal background" |
| Show freshness | steam, glossy sauce, visible crisp crust |

And §17.6 Don'ts, written into every prompt as explicit negatives: *not
oversaturated, no artificial gloss, no over-editing, natural appearance.*

Two additions of our own:

- **No text, no logos, no watermarks.** A model left to itself will invent
  branding, and an invented bb.q mark is worse than no mark.
- **No hands or faces**, except where a shot is deliberately an action shot
  (§17.4). A generated hand is the fastest way to make food look unreal.

**One rule the prompts must never break:** bb.q Chicken is *twice fried in
olive oil and tossed to order*. Nothing is grilled, seared or flame-cooked, and
that vocabulary is banned brand-wide — `npm run brand:check` fails the build on
it. Every prompt below says "twice-fried" and "crisp", never anything hotter.

## Putting a result into the site

1. Save the chosen frame as `assets/food/masters/<imageKey>.jpg`.
2. Point the product's `imageKey` in `infra/seed/products.ts` at it.
3. `npm run assets:derive` regenerates the 4:5 and 16:9 crops.
4. `npm run demo:build` rebuilds the single-file review build. It refuses any
   product whose `imageKey` has no master, so a typo fails loudly.
5. Remove the item from the awaiting-photography note in `apps/web/README.md`.

`tests/catalogue.test.ts` asserts every master on disk is used by something, so
a master added and never pointed at will fail the suite.
