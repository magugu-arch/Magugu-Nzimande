Option 01

01

S + T monogram

Soweto, bridged to Towers.

An S drawn from two circles. Its top stroke runs straight on to become the T’s crossbar, the bridge, and the T’s stem in red is the drop, stopping short of the ground. One continuous line, one weight, round ends. The name sits beside it in widely spaced capitals.

Strength

Ownable initials that work as an app icon, a stamp, embroidery and a wristband. It reads as letters first, so it travels well beyond the site.

Watch out

It doesn’t picture the towers, so until people know it the name sits beside it.

Option 02

02

Two T’s

Two T’s, two towers.

There are exactly two T’s in Soweto Towers, and the towers stand in them. Each stem curves in to a waist and flares to its base like a cooling tower, and the crossbar rests on it with one fine cut, like the bridge on a rim. The name is the logo.

Strength

The quietest and most timeless of the three. Every use builds recognition of the name itself, and it sits comfortably next to partners and sponsors.

Watch out

The idea is subtle at small sizes. The app icon uses the two tower-T’s on their own.

Option 03

03

The Loop

Seen from above.

From the air, the two towers are two rims and the bridge runs between them. The Loop draws that view in one red line, with one cut where the line meets itself. It also reads as a loop without end, and turned upright it becomes the S of Soweto.

Strength

The boldest and simplest: one colour, one line, instantly memorable. The upright S makes a strong app icon, and the Loop repeats into a pattern for wristbands, packaging and the web.

Watch out

It is abstract: people learn it rather than recognise the towers at first sight. Infinity-like shapes are common in logos, so it should always appear with the name.
