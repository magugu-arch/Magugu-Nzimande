Soweto TowersIdentity · Logo proposal
Orlando East, SowetoNovember 2026 · Version 1.0

01 The idea

Look between the towers.

The Orlando cooling towers are Soweto’s best-known skyline. The mark draws them as they are, two true hyperboloids, and lets the space between them do the talking. The suspension bridge across their tops is the crossbar. The 100 m drop from it is the stem. Together they spell T, for Towers.

++=

A landmark, a letter and a leap, in one shape.

02 Construction

Drawn like the real thing.

  1. True hyperboloid

    Each tower follows the curve of a real cooling tower: a wide base (B), a throat (a) at 73% of its height, and a slight flare at the rim. A hyperboloid is made entirely of straight lines, shown in the drawing.

  2. One cut

    A single cut width (s) separates every part: the bridge from the towers, and the towers from the drop. It is what turns two shapes into a letter.

  3. The drop stops short

    The stem tapers to a point just above the ground, like the cord at full stretch.

  4. Built square

    The bridge spans exactly the two tower bases (W = 4B + 2s). The mark sits on a flat baseline and a flat top, so it aligns cleanly with type, grids and edges.

03 The logo suite

One mark, four lockups.

Primary · Horizontal Default

The two lines of the wordmark share one width, and their O, W and E sit in the same columns. The type spans the tower height so the bridge stands over the name.

Stacked

Square and portrait formats: social, posters, merchandise.

Stacked + descriptor

Where people meet the brand for the first time: signage, print, press.

Mark

App icon, favicon, wristbands, stamps, and anywhere the name is already present.

Wordmark

Narrow horizontal spaces, and as a sign-off where the mark already appears nearby.

04 Colour versions

Red is for the drop.

In full colour the T is always Drop Red. Where only one colour is possible, the whole logo goes to that colour and the cuts still draw the T.

05 Clear space & size

Give it room.

Keep a clear space of x on every side, where x is the width of one tower base (about half the mark’s width). Nothing else enters that space: no type, edges or other logos.

07 Typography

One family, two widths.

Archivo is a free, open-source typeface (SIL Open Font License) on Google Fonts. A single variable file holds both the expanded display cut used in the wordmark and the regular text cut, so the website loads one font.
