Soweto TowersIdentity guidelines
Orlando East, SowetoNovember 2026 · Version 2.0

01 The idea

Two towers. One bridge. One jump.

The Orlando cooling towers are Soweto’s best-known skyline. The mark draws them exactly as they stand, joined by the bridge across their rims. Hanging from it is the only colour in the logo: the cord and the jumper, the moment the place is known for.

++=

Everything is still, except the one thing that moves.

02 Construction

Drawn like the real thing.

  1. True hyperboloids

    Each tower follows the curve of a real cooling tower: a wide base, a throat at 76% of its height, and a slight flare at the rim. A hyperboloid is made entirely of straight lines, shown in the drawing.

  2. One unit

    Every measure is a ratio of B, half a tower base. The towers are 3B tall, stand 1.24B apart, and the bridge, the rim and the cord are fixed fractions of B. Scale it to any size and nothing drifts.

  3. The rim

    A fine cut below each rim is the detail that makes these cooling towers and not columns. The bridge springs from the rims, level with the top of the mark.

  4. The jump

    The cord drops from the exact centre of the bridge, and the jumper hangs at half the towers’ height. It is the only colour in the mark.

03 The logo

Quiet, so the towers speak.

The name is set in widely spaced capitals at a modest weight, so the mark carries the weight and the name carries the calm.

05 Clear space & size

Give it room.

Keep a clear space of x on every side, where x is B, half the width of a tower base. Nothing else enters that space: no type, edges or other logos.

Mark, small-size master16–48 px · 4–12 mm

Small-size master

Below 48 px the cord and the rim cut become too fine to print or render. The small-size master drops them, thickens the bridge and enlarges the jumper, so the idea survives in a browser tab.

07 Typography

One family, used with restraint.

Archivo is free and open source (SIL Open Font License) on Google Fonts. One variable file covers the name, the headlines and the text, so the website loads a single font.
